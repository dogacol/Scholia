Supposing truth is a woman—what then? Are there not grounds for suspecting that all philosophers, in so far as they have been dogmatists, have failed to understand women—that the ghastly earnestness and the clumsy importunity with which they have usually paid their addresses to Truth, have been unskilled and unseemly methods for winning a woman?

After all, when a dogmatist is confronted with a contradiction, with a reality that refuses to fit within his formula, he usually behaves as though a personal insult had been offered to him; he stamps and he writhes. One must admire the pride with which he still clings to his creed, yet it is pride of the sort that knows not how to blush.

It may be that the dogmatist will raise a shield of logic and scholasticism between himself and that which wounds him; but the spear of experience will pierce it. For our own part, let us be on our guard against the dangerous old conceptions of “pure reason,” “absolute spirituality,” and “knowledge in itself”: they demand of us that we should adore mere mummies.
